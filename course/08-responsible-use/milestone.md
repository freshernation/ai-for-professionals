# Module 8 milestone: your rules, your numbers and your plan

This is the last milestone of the course. It brings together what you will carry forward.

## What to produce

Three short documents, each no longer than a page.

**1. Your working rules**

- Your three-line checking rule: what you glance at, what you check, and what you verify, with an example of each from your own work.
- What may and may not go into which tool, with three examples from the information you actually handle.
- The actions an assistant may never take for you without your approval.
- Where your organisation's policy is, and who you ask when you are unsure.

If your team has no agreed rules, add a one-page draft of answers to the five questions (tools, information, checking, saying so, and what it is not used for) to take to your team.

**2. Your numbers**

- A time log, started and kept for at least one week before the session, and continued to two.
- Your guess, written down before you began, of how many hours a week AI saves you.
- Your tasks sorted into clear wins, marginal and losses, as far as the log shows so far.

**3. Your 90-day plan**

- Three tasks that are frequent, suited to AI and yours to change.
- A milestone for day 30, day 60 and day 90, each something you will produce or do.
- The dates in your calendar, and the name of the person you have told.
- Your one source for keeping up, and the date of your first monthly half-hour.

Bring all three to the final session, together with the goal you set on the "Start here" page at the beginning of the course.

## How you know it is good

- Your checking rule uses real examples, and you have applied it to at least one piece of work this week.
- You have read your organisation's AI policy, or you have asked in writing and can show the question.
- Your log includes at least one task where AI did not help.
- You can state what you counted as "time with AI", and it includes checking and fixing.
- You can name one thing you have stopped using AI for, or decided not to start, and say why.
- Each of your three milestones could be shown to someone else as done or not done.
- The plan fits the time you really have.
- Someone other than you knows about the plan.
- You can say, in two sentences, how far you have come towards the goal you set at the start, and what the next step is.
