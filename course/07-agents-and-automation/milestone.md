# Module 7 milestone: delegate one real task, safely

## What to produce

Hand one real, multi-step task to an agent, from brief to reviewed result, and write a one-page record of it. Then draw up your own list of what an assistant may and may not do on your behalf.

Before you start, confirm that the tool and any connection you use are approved by your employer. If agent features are not available to you at work, do the exercise with a read-only research task on public information.

**Part A: a delegated task**

1. Choose a repetitive, multi-step task that only reads, gathers, sorts or drafts. Nothing in it should send, pay, delete or publish.
2. Write a brief with all seven parts: goal, what done looks like, inputs, method, limits, checkpoints and the report.
3. Ask for the plan. Question one awkward case.
4. Run a pilot on three items and check them by hand. Amend the brief.
5. Run the full task. Watch the start.
6. Review: the report, the counts, every exception, a spot-check of the unremarkable, and whether anything else changed.

**Part B: your rules**

1. List the actions an assistant could take in the tools you use.
2. For each, answer: can it be undone, and who is affected?
3. Mark each one allowed, needs approval, or not permitted.
4. Check your actual settings against the list and correct them.

Optional: set up one scheduled task that reads and reports, with a rule for "nothing to report" and a proof line. Check its first three runs.

Bring to the module session:

- Your brief, as first written and as amended.
- What the plan or the pilot revealed.
- The counts from your review, and one error you found.
- Your table of actions and decisions.
- Your list of connections and scheduled tasks, and anything you switched off.

## How you know it is good

- The task was real and you would otherwise have done it by hand.
- The agent could not send, pay, delete or publish anything during the task. This was true because of what it could do, as well as what it was told.
- Your brief states limits, checkpoints and what the report must contain.
- The pilot changed the brief at least once.
- Your review accounts for every item, and you checked items the agent reported as fine.
- You can name an error the agent made and say how you caught it.
- No write action in your settings happens without your approval.
- For anything that reads outside content, you can say which of the three conditions is absent.
- You know where to see what an agent has done on your behalf, and how to stop one.
- You have removed at least one connection or scheduled task you were not using, or confirmed you have none.
