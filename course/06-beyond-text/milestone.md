# Module 6 milestone: make one thing that isn't a document

## What to produce

Build **one small working tool** for a real task, and **one other thing** of your choice: an image, a narration, a short video or a web page. Both should be for something you actually need at work. Nothing confidential or personal should go into either.

**Part A: a working tool (everyone)**

1. Choose a calculation, checklist or conversion that you or your team do by hand.
2. Before building, work out four test cases by hand, including one unusual case.
3. Describe the tool: who uses it, inputs, output, rules and one worked example.
4. Build it in small steps. Run your tests. Report failures precisely and re-run everything after each fix.
5. Try to break it, and have one other person use it without explanation.
6. Answer the four questions: where the data goes, who can open it, what people will type into it, and what happens if it is wrong.

**Part B: one of these**

- **An image** for a slide, document or invitation: described properly, improved in three steps, checked for text, detail and accuracy.
- **A narration** of a short script: rewritten for the ear, produced with a stock voice, and listened to in full.
- **A short video** of about ninety seconds: script and shot list first, real footage where it matters, captioned and reviewed with sound on and off.
- **A web page** with one purpose: built from your real content, with everything invented removed.

Bring to the module session:

- Your tool, working, with your table of test cases and results.
- The test that failed, if one did, and what the fault was.
- Your Part B piece.
- Your answers to the rights and disclosure questions for it: do you need to own it, could it resemble someone else's work, does it show or imitate a real person, could it be mistaken for real, and how is it labelled?

## How you know it is good

- The tool does a job that somebody really has.
- Your test cases were worked out by hand before you built it, and include an unusual case.
- Every test passes, and you re-ran all of them after the last change.
- You can say where the tool's data goes and who can open it.
- You decided who the tool is for: you alone, your team, or a prototype for IT. You can give the reason.
- Nothing personal or confidential was typed into either piece.
- Your Part B piece contains no invented facts, figures, quotations or people presented as real.
- No real person's face or voice was used without their agreement.
- Anything that could be mistaken for real is labelled.
- You would be comfortable if the audience knew exactly how each piece was made.
