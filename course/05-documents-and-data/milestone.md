# Module 5 milestone: one document and one dataset, understood and checked

## What to produce

Two short pieces of work on real material from your job. Before you begin, confirm that each file may go into the tool you are using. If it may not, use a version with the sensitive parts removed, or a different file.

**Part A: a document**

1. Choose a long document you need to understand, or a set of two to five that you need to compare.
2. Ask for a summary or comparison built around your own questions, with section or page numbers and exact quotes for obligations, deadlines, limits and amounts.
3. Spot-check the three points that matter most against the original.
4. Ask what was left out and what is unusual.

**Part B: a dataset**

1. Choose a spreadsheet you work with.
2. Ask the assistant to describe it before changing anything. Clean one thing, with a list of changes.
3. Ask one analysis question. Confirm the rows analysed, check one number by hand, and check that the totals add up.
4. Make one chart whose title states its finding. Check two values against the data, and ask how it could mislead.

Bring to the module session:

- Your summary, with the three points you checked marked right or wrong.
- One thing the summary left out or got wrong, and how you found it.
- Your chart, with its title.
- The number you checked by hand, and whether it matched.
- One assumption the assistant made about your data that you had not expected.

## How you know it is good

- Both files were allowed in the tool, or were stripped down first.
- The summary answers your questions, and every point has a section or page reference.
- You opened the original for at least three points. You can say what you found.
- You know how many rows the assistant analysed, and it matches your file.
- At least one figure was confirmed by your own filter or sum in the spreadsheet.
- The chart makes one point, its title states that point, and bars start at zero.
- You can say whether the chart should show counts or rates, and why.
- You can name something the assistant assumed about blanks, duplicates or categories.
- Nothing from either piece of work would embarrass you if a colleague asked "where does that come from?".
